  1. 01Field-wide · security

    SAP confirms a cyberattack on its development systems

    What happenedSAP said it is investigating a security incident in its development infrastructure and has found no sign that customer business data was accessed. Der Spiegel, citing insiders, reported that attackers used stolen GitHub login tokens of SAP employees to copy and change code. Handelsblatt reports that SAP replaced its code-signing keys, limited GitHub access and paused automated development work.

    Why it mattersSAP software runs the finance, HR and supply chains of most large organisations, so any change to its code is a supply-chain risk; it is the second big case this week (after 1Password) of attackers entering through GitHub accounts.

    Note: it is not confirmed that private code was taken or that changed code reached customers. Reports that AI helped the attack are not confirmed.

    Source: SecurityWeek · 27 Nov 2026

  2. 02Information systems · data governance

    Google: from February, AI agents may reach Workspace data only through Google's MCP servers

    What happenedGoogle's developer page says that from 1 February 2027, AI agents must use the Google Workspace MCP servers or the Workspace CLI to work with Gmail, Drive, Calendar and other data; agents that call the normal REST APIs directly will be limited to the lowest quota. Google argues that the REST APIs were built for human-paced apps, while its MCP servers add permission checks, data loss prevention, human confirmation for sensitive actions and prompt-injection screening. New terms also forbid agents from scraping the APIs or keeping some data "for extended periods", and paid usage tiers start in April 2027.

    Why it mattersThis is a clear example of a platform setting rules for machine access to personal and organisational data, and it will affect every university and company that connects AI assistants to Google Workspace.

    Note: announced on 24–25 November; the official page was last updated on 28 November. "Extended periods" is not defined. Claims that the Gmail MCP server cannot send email are not confirmed.

    Source: Google for Developers · updated 28 Nov 2026, and Infosecurity Magazine · date not shown

  3. 03Field-wide · e-government

    UK benefits department takes online services offline after a "cyber incident"

    What happenedOn Friday the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) said it had taken some online services offline as a precaution after detecting a cyber incident. According to ITV, applying for Universal Credit, signing in to accounts and job search appeared to be unavailable. DWP says benefit and pension payments are not affected and that it is working with the National Cyber Security Centre.

    Why it mattersWhen a core public information system goes down, millions of people lose access to essential services, which shows why resilience and offline alternatives must be part of e-government design.

    Note: DWP has not said which services are down, when they will return, or whether data was taken. ITV reported no evidence of stolen personal data as of Friday morning.

    Source: The Register · 27 Nov 2026, and ITV News · 27 Nov 2026

  4. 04Artificial intelligence · regulation

    Japan plans binding safety duties and penalties for frontier AI providers

    What happenedA Cabinet Office draft would amend Japan's AI Promotion Act, which today has no penalties. Frontier AI providers would have to refuse help with cyberattacks and weapons, act against likely malfunctions, and report accidents and exploitable flaws; enforcement would go from a recommendation to a binding order, with a penalty only if the order is broken. Foreign providers used in Japan, such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba and DeepSeek, are expected to be covered, and the bill is planned for the 2027 Diet session.

    Why it mattersJapan has been a "soft law" country for AI; a move to binding rules means that, alongside the EU AI Act, another major market will require incident reporting from model providers.

    Note: this is a draft. Penalty amounts are not decided; reported fines of ¥100 million are not confirmed. Identity checks for users of cyber-capable models are only mentioned in one report (Yomiuri).

    Source: Asia Daily · 27 Nov 2026, and Tech Times (citing Yomiuri) · 26 Nov 2026

  5. 05Field-wide · jobs

    ECB survey: 44% of euro-area workers use AI at work at least once a week

    What happenedA European Central Bank blog post, based on its Consumer Expectations Survey in 11 countries, says more than half of workers used AI at work in May 2026, and in August 44% used it weekly and about 18% daily. Most users say AI helps them finish more work, often by finding and processing information, but checking and correcting AI output takes time. Workers expect 46% of people in their sector to use AI within five years, and many more people expect a higher risk of job loss than higher pay.

    Why it mattersThese are solid, large-scale numbers on real AI use at work, useful for teaching and for planning which information skills (searching, checking, evaluating) students need.

    Note: the blog does not give the sample size; the link between job-loss fears and support for regulation is descriptive, not causal.

    Source: ECB Blog · 27 Nov 2026

  6. 06Artificial intelligence · chips

    Nvidia Blackwell racks found at a Shanghai data centre linked to Megaspeed

    What happenedBloomberg found loan documents in which two Shanghai companies pledged Nvidia GB300 and GB200 servers as collateral, at a site called the "Yangtze River Delta Project". Blackwell chips need a US export licence for China. One company says it serves Megaspeed, which was added to a US trade blacklist this month, and the other is majority-owned by a firm linked to Megaspeed's founder Alice Huang.

    Why it mattersIt shows how hard it is to control where AI hardware ends up, at a time when Nvidia is already under close scrutiny in Washington.

    Note: it is not known how the hardware got there; no evidence shows Megaspeed shipped it, and Megaspeed denies wrongdoing.

    Source: Investing.com via Yahoo Finance (citing Bloomberg) · 27 Nov 2026

  7. 07Field-wide · big companies

    OpenAI countersues Apple, alleging trade-secret theft and pressure on suppliers

    What happenedOpenAI filed a counterclaim in California against Apple, which sued OpenAI and its io hardware unit in July over hardware secrets. OpenAI claims Apple stole its trade secrets, threatened suppliers that work with OpenAI, and is trying to "compete unfairly by any means available". Apple's case is set for trial in July 2027.

    Why it mattersThe fight over AI devices is moving into the courts and the supply chain, which could slow new AI hardware and shape how staff mobility and trade secrets are handled in tech.

    Note: these are allegations only; Apple had not responded in the report.

    Source: 9to5Mac · 27 Nov 2026

  8. 08Information systems · e-commerce

    Black Friday: AI shopping agents made only 0.4% of online orders, and many shops block them

    What happenedBain & Co estimates that AI agents made 0.4% of global online transactions and 0.2% of their value. Amazon blocks Meta's Muse agent and is suing Perplexity, while eBay, United Airlines and Kohl's restrict agents; a Bloomberg-reported test found about half of big retailers do not let AI assistants shop. In a Visa test of 22 models on 15 shopping tasks, the agents failed 42.8% of runs, and in more than half of the failures they still bought something.

    Why it mattersAgent-based shopping is still tiny and unreliable, and the rules for whether machines may act for users on websites are being decided now, a core question for information systems and UX.

    Note: Bain's methods were not described in the report.

    Source: The Next Web · 27 Nov 2026

  9. 09Artificial intelligence · ethics · education

    Michael Nielsen takes down his free deep learning textbook in protest at frontier AI labs

    What happenedMichael Nielsen removed "Neural Networks and Deep Learning", the free online book he wrote in 2011–2015 and that many courses used. The site now shows only an essay in which he says he does not want to help train people for frontier AI work, and that the labs are knowingly putting humanity at risk; he calls current safety promises "safety theatre". The move drew wide discussion on Hacker News.

    Why it mattersA standard open teaching resource has disappeared overnight, a reminder for teachers to keep local copies of open material and an example of researchers' growing public protest against frontier AI.

    Note: based on one report; we could not open Nielsen's own post. Anthropic and OpenAI had not commented.

    Source: Mad Robot · 27 Nov 2026

  2. ۰۲سیستم‌های اطلاعاتی · حکمرانی داده

    چه اتفاقی افتادصفحهٔ توسعه‌دهندگان Google می‌گوید از ۱ فوریهٔ ۲۰۲۷ عامل‌های هوش مصنوعی برای کار با دادهٔ Gmail، Drive، Calendar و دیگر سرویس‌ها باید از سرورهای MCP یا CLI مخصوص Google Workspace استفاده کنند؛ عامل‌هایی که مستقیم REST API معمولی را صدا بزنند به پایین‌ترین سهمیه محدود می‌شوند. Google می‌گوید REST API برای برنامه‌هایی با سرعت کار انسان ساخته شده بود، در حالی که سرورهای MCP بررسی مجوز، جلوگیری از نشت داده، تأیید انسانی برای کارهای حساس و غربال حملهٔ prompt injection دارند. شرایط تازه همچنین خراشیدن (scraping) API و نگه‌داشتن برخی داده‌ها «برای مدت طولانی» را ممنوع می‌کند و سطح‌های پولی استفاده از آوریل ۲۰۲۷ آغاز می‌شود.

    چرا مهم استاین نمونهٔ روشنی است از اینکه یک سکو قواعد دسترسی ماشین‌ها به دادهٔ شخصی و سازمانی را تعیین می‌کند، و بر هر دانشگاه و شرکتی که دستیارهای هوش مصنوعی را به Google Workspace وصل می‌کند اثر می‌گذارد.

    توجه: این خبر در ۲۴ و ۲۵ نوامبر اعلام شد؛ صفحهٔ رسمی آخرین بار در ۲۸ نوامبر به‌روز شده است. «مدت طولانی» تعریف نشده است. ادعای اینکه سرور MCP مربوط به Gmail نمی‌تواند ایمیل بفرستد تأیید نشده است.

    منبع: Google for Developers · به‌روزشده در ۲۸ نوامبر ۲۰۲۶، و Infosecurity Magazine · تاریخ نشان داده نشده
