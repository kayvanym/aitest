  1. 01Privacy · data governance

    42 US states try to stop the auction of 21 million Character.AI users' private chats

    What happenedA coalition of 42 state attorneys general has stepped into Character.AI's Chapter 11 bankruptcy case to block the sale of user data. They say the data covers more than 21 million US accounts, about 2.1 million of them registered to minors, including chat histories and IP addresses; the filing says some chats contain mental and medical health disclosures. The only known bidder is a Delaware company formed on 13 November, and the auction is set for 1 December.

    Why it mattersThis is a test of whether a privacy promise survives bankruptcy: if user data is treated as an asset that can be sold, a new owner may not be bound by the old privacy policy.

    Note: the states' claims are allegations in a court filing. Character.AI says user information stays protected under its privacy policy. TechCrunch and Gizmodo differ slightly on when the court approved the lead bidder.

    Source: TechCrunch · 25 Nov 2026, and Gizmodo · 25 Nov 2026

  2. 02Field-wide · law

    Australia passes a "digital duty of care" law that covers apps, games and AI chatbots

    What happenedAustralia's Parliament passed the Digital Duty of Care Act, which requires online services, including AI chatbots, to actively reduce the risk of serious harm to users. After a deal with the Greens, the maximum fine was raised from A$109 million to 5% of a company's global revenue; the Senate vote was 36 to 23. The opposition called the law "authoritarian" and promised to repeal it, and the US ambassador said it would push platforms to censor lawful speech.

    Why it mattersLike the EU Digital Services Act and the UK Online Safety Act, the law shifts responsibility for harm onto system design, so risk assessment becomes part of building any information service used in Australia.

    Source: Associated Press via Fast Company · 25 Nov 2026

  3. 03Artificial intelligence · security

    Microsoft: Chinese and North Korean state hackers now use the AI attack agent ARTEX

    What happenedIn a threat report on 25 November, Microsoft said state-backed groups including China's Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon and North Korea's Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet have added ARTEX to their tools. ARTEX is an open-source AI agent for penetration testing that chains known tools to scan systems, guess passwords and find exposed secrets much faster than a human. Its original code was removed from GitHub, but copies and a paid version from a Chinese firm are still available, and Microsoft advises spotting it by its unusual speed of activity.

    Why it mattersAI agents mainly make old weaknesses (weak passwords, exposed keys, unpatched web apps) easier to exploit at scale, so basic security hygiene in university and company systems matters more than ever.

    Note: we could not open Microsoft's report itself; details come from The Record's report (via Techmeme) and an AI-assisted security news site. Microsoft does not say what each group did with the tool.

    Source: The Record via Techmeme · 26 Nov 2026, and i10x · 25 Nov 2026

  4. 04Artificial intelligence · law

    New York bans toys with built-in AI chatbots for five years

    What happenedGovernor Kathy Hochul signed a law that stops the making, distribution and sale of toys with embedded AI chatbots in New York for five years. It takes effect 90 days after signing, so not during this holiday season, and the attorney general can seek fines of up to $15,000 per violation per day. The state cites tests in which some chatbot toys for children aged 3 to 12 discussed sexual topics and could record voices or scan faces.

    Why it mattersAfter California's four-year ban in September, US states are now regulating AI by product type and audience, which matters for anyone designing conversational systems that children might use.

    Note: this is the state's own press release; the testing organisation is not named in it.

    Source: Office of the Governor of New York · 24 Nov 2026

  5. 05Field-wide · AI hardware

    Nvidia reportedly freezes AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries

    What happenedAccording to the Financial Times, Nvidia told server makers such as Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping AI servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The trigger is new US Commerce Department rules that give regional cloud providers 90 days to get an export licence by proving that restricted Chinese companies are not among their customers. Lenders have also paused financing for data centres linked to Chinese cloud firms.

    Why it mattersSoutheast Asia is a major hub for rented AI computing; export rules now decide where AI capacity can be built and who can use it, which affects cost and access for researchers worldwide.

    Note: this is based on unnamed sources; Nvidia declined to comment, and it is unclear whether the freeze is temporary.

    Source: Malay Mail, reporting the Financial Times · 26 Nov 2026

  6. 06Security · data protection

    1Password says hackers got into its internal systems through an employee's GitHub account

    What happenedThe password manager 1Password said that on 19 November it detected an attacker who had taken over an employee's corporate GitHub account and used it to push malicious code to internal servers. The attacker downloaded customer names, sign-in email addresses and account creation and last-login dates. The company says customer vaults were never accessed and that it does not hold master passwords or secret keys.

    Why it mattersA developer account became the way into a security company's back end; software supply chains and code-hosting accounts are now a core part of data governance.

    Note: it is not yet known how the employee account was compromised or how many customers are affected.

    Source: TechCrunch · 25 Nov 2026

  7. 07Field-wide · infrastructure

    US regulator lets SpaceX launch up to one million satellites as data centres in space

    What happenedThe Federal Communications Commission (FCC) authorised SpaceX's "Starmind" network of up to one million satellites for data processing in orbit, which its chairman called the first US approval of data centres in space. The first satellite must work by 2033 and the full network by 2040, with conditions including a debris plan. The FCC rejected objections from Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society.

    Why it mattersThis is a permit, not a working system, but it shows how the hunger for AI computing is pushing infrastructure, and questions of governance, into new places.

    Note: scientists question the cooling, cost, space debris and harm to astronomy; no Starmind satellite is in orbit yet.

    Source: GeekWire · 25 Nov 2026

  8. 08Artificial intelligence · software engineering

    Anthropic used Claude agents to rewrite its main API in Rust, checked against live traffic

    What happenedAnthropic described moving its Messages API, the main way customers reach Claude, from Python to Rust, reporting about 70% less CPU per request and about 20% faster time to first token. Claude wrote a first version in about a week, but the rewritten code passed 98% of the old tests while matching Python on only about a quarter of real inputs. The team then compared both versions on live traffic, logging only the shape of each difference for privacy, with agents fixing mismatches under human review until 99.6% matched.

    Why it mattersA clear case study for teaching: passing tests is not the same as correct behaviour, and checking AI-written code against real use, with human review, is what made the migration safe.

    Note: the performance numbers are the company's own.

    Source: Anthropic Engineering · 25 Nov 2026

  9. 09Artificial intelligence · tools

    Claude can now install artifacts as desktop apps on Mac and Windows

    What happenedAnthropic launched an "Install as app" beta that turns a Claude artifact into an app with its own window and Dock or taskbar icon. Users choose one of three permission levels, from browser-level access up to full computer access, which is allowed only for apps the user or their organisation built; permissions must be granted again after every update. Apps use the person's Claude account and usage limits, and Anthropic does not review apps before they are installed.

    Why it mattersSmall, AI-built personal tools are becoming installable software, which raises new questions about permissions, trust and who maintains these apps inside organisations.

    Source: Anthropic help article · 24 Nov 2026, and MacRumors · 25 Nov 2026

ARTEX یک عامل هوش مصنوعی متن‌باز برای آزمون نفوذ است که ابزارهای شناخته‌شده را به هم زنجیر می‌کند تا سیستم‌ها را پویش کند، گذرواژه‌ها را حدس بزند و کلیدهای افشاشده را پیدا کند، بسیار سریع‌تر از یک انسان. کد اصلی آن از GitHub حذف شده، اما نسخه‌هایی از آن و یک نسخهٔ پولی از یک شرکت چینی همچنان در دسترس است، و Microsoft توصیه می‌کند آن را از روی سرعت غیرعادی فعالیتش شناسایی کنند.

    چرا مهم استعامل‌های هوش مصنوعی بیشتر بهره‌برداری از ضعف‌های قدیمی (گذرواژهٔ ضعیف، کلیدهای افشاشده، اپلیکیشن‌های وصله‌نشده) را در مقیاس بزرگ آسان می‌کنند؛ پس بهداشت امنیتی پایه در سیستم‌های دانشگاه‌ها و شرکت‌ها از همیشه مهم‌تر است.

    توجه: نتوانستیم خود گزارش Microsoft را باز کنیم؛ جزئیات از گزارش The Record (از طریق Techmeme) و یک سایت خبری امنیتی با کمک هوش مصنوعی است. Microsoft نمی‌گوید هر گروه با این ابزار چه کرده است.

    منبع: The Record از طریق Techmeme · ۲۶ نوامبر ۲۰۲۶، و i10x · ۲۵ نوامبر ۲۰۲۶

    چرا مهم استپس از ممنوعیت چهارسالهٔ کالیفرنیا در سپتامبر، ایالت‌های آمریکا اکنون هوش مصنوعی را بر پایهٔ نوع محصول و مخاطب تنظیم می‌کنند؛ این برای هر کسی که سیستم‌های گفت‌وگومحور طراحی می‌کند که کودکان ممکن است از آن استفاده کنند مهم است.

    توجه: این اطلاعیهٔ خود ایالت است و نام سازمان انجام‌دهندهٔ آزمون در آن نیامده است.

  6. ۰۶امنیت · حفاظت از داده
