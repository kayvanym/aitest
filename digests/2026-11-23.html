Overview

The weekend's biggest story was in education: South Korea's education minister resigned after weeks of controversy over AI-assisted cheating around the national university entrance exam. The AI prompt-leak story moved forward: OpenRouter removed three inference providers after the Dragos findings, and Together AI disclosed its own prompt leak caused by a credential that a coding agent had exposed. In the US, governors asked for federal rules on the most powerful AI systems that still leave room for state laws, and big banks are blocking third-party AI agents from customer accounts. On the research side, a new test shows coding agents failing most tasks in a simulated workplace.

We found no important new story in the last 24 hours on the EU AI Act, GDPR enforcement or the job market. Several original pages (Bloomberg, Wired, The Guardian and others) could not be opened from our side today, so some items rely on short summaries of them; we say so in the notes.

News

  1. 01Field-wide · education

    South Korea's education minister resigns over AI cheating fears around the university entrance exam

    What happenedEducation Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigned on Sunday 22 November, and President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation shortly after. The pressure built after Choi told parliament on 16 November that it would be hard to catch all AI-assisted cheating, and he refused to postpone the national entrance exam (Suneung), which about 550,000 students sat on 19 November under phone bans and camera checks. An opposition politician had also filed a police complaint against him for dereliction of duty.

    Why it mattersA minister losing his post over AI and exam integrity shows how urgently universities need assessment methods that still work when every student has an AI assistant.

    Note: we could not open The Guardian article itself; the summary combines its short description with Korean reports. The police complaint is an allegation, not a finding.

    Source: The Guardian · 22 Nov 2026, and The Korea Herald (Yonhap) · 17 Nov 2026

  2. 02Privacy · data governance

    Prompt-leak follow-up: OpenRouter drops three AI providers, and Together AI reports its own leak

    What happenedAfter the Dragos "canary" study we reported yesterday, the AI routing service OpenRouter removed three inference providers and 125 endpoints from its "zero data retention" list and told affected customers. Separately, Together AI disclosed that an outsider downloaded stored samples of some customer prompts from its serverless inference service, using a credential that an AI coding agent had exposed.

    Why it mattersThis turns a research claim into concrete action by platforms: "zero data retention" labels are now being checked and withdrawn, and AI coding agents appear as a new source of leaked credentials, two lessons for anyone who governs data in AI systems.

    Note: we read these reports through short summaries and could not check the original OpenRouter and Together AI statements. The names of the three providers were not confirmed.

    Source: The Hacker News · 22 Nov 2026, and Unite.AI · 21 Nov 2026

  3. 03Field-wide · regulation

    US governors ask for federal rules on the most powerful AI, but without blocking state laws

    What happenedA bipartisan group of US governors asked Washington to set national rules for the most powerful AI systems. At the same time, they want states to keep the power to protect consumers, workers and children with their own laws, instead of being overruled by a federal ban on state AI rules.

    Why it mattersWhether the US ends up with one federal AI law or a patchwork of state laws decides which rules on transparency, data use and accountability information systems will have to follow.

    Note: the Bloomberg article is paywalled; we could only read its short summary, so details of the governors' proposal are not confirmed.

    Source: Bloomberg · 22 Nov 2026

  4. 04Artificial intelligence · research

    Coding agents pass isolated tests but finish fewer than one in ten tasks in a simulated workplace

    What happenedA new benchmark puts AI coding agents into a simulated developer job, with colleagues, ticket boards and handoffs between people. Even the best current models closed fewer than one in ten tickets, although they do very well on the usual isolated coding tests.

    Why it mattersIt shows the gap between benchmark scores and real organisational work, where communication, context and coordination matter; this is exactly the area that information systems research studies.

    Note: we could not open the underlying paper, so the benchmark's name and exact numbers come from The Decoder's report.

    Source: The Decoder · 22 Nov 2026

  5. 05Field-wide · big company decisions

    Large US banks block third-party AI agents from logging into customer accounts

    What happenedMajor US banks are stopping outside AI bots from logging into customers' online banking. They also say that if a customer lets an AI agent act for them, costly mistakes by that agent are the customer's responsibility.

    Why it mattersAs AI agents start to act on people's behalf, questions of identity, authorisation and liability move to the centre of system design; banks are among the first to draw hard lines.

    Note: we could only read a short summary of the Wired report; which banks are involved was not confirmed. An earlier digest (19 Nov) covered a related Arizona investigation.

    Source: Wired · 22 Nov 2026

  6. 06Information retrieval · NLP

    spaCy's makers show coding agents and open models building named-entity training data cheaply

    What happenedExplosion, the company behind the spaCy library, described a workflow in which a coding agent (such as Claude Code) and several open-weight models together label text for named-entity recognition, using agreement between the models as a quality check. The company says the resulting data costs less and is of higher quality than labels from one large commercial model.

    Why it mattersEntity extraction is a core step in search, digital libraries and metadata work; cheap, checkable training data could make custom extractors practical for small projects and teaching.

    Note: the quality claims are the company's own and have not been independently tested.

    Source: Explosion blog · 22 Nov 2026

  7. 07Artificial intelligence · new models

    India's Sarvam AI releases an open-weight 105-billion-parameter model for business agents

    What happenedSarvam AI released Sarvam-105B as open weights, aimed at customer-support and voice agents. It comes as full-precision weights for further training and as a compressed 4-bit version that the company says runs on a single workstation GPU.

    Why it mattersLarge open models that run on local hardware let universities and organisations keep sensitive data in-house, which matters for privacy and data sovereignty outside the US.

    Note: performance claims come from the company and were not independently checked.

    Source: Unite.AI · 22 Nov 2026

  8. 08Artificial intelligence · tools

    Google previews "tool retrieval" in the Gemini API, so agents load tools only when needed

    What happenedGoogle added a preview feature to the Gemini API that lets an agent keep a large library of tools available but load each tool's description only when it is needed, inside a single request. This avoids filling the model's context with hundreds of tool definitions.

    Why it mattersChoosing the right tool from a large library is itself a retrieval problem, so classic information retrieval ideas are moving into the design of AI agents.

    Note: we found only a secondary report and no Google announcement; the feature is in preview.

    Source: MarkTechPost · 22 Nov 2026
