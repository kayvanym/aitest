  1. 01Field-wide · market

    Anthropic's first trading day: shares open 34% above the IPO price, then give back much of the gain

    What happenedAnthropic's shares began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on 19 November. The first trade was at $111.50, about 34% above the $83 IPO price, but the price fell below $100 within minutes and was around $96 by early afternoon New York time, on a day when the wider market fell about 1.5%. Including a separate $26 billion private placement, Anthropic raised about $107 billion in total.

    Why it mattersHow the market values the first big AI model company to list will shape funding, prices and competition for the AI tools that universities and organisations depend on.

    Note: we could not confirm the closing price. Reports also disagree on the company's value, from about $1.3 trillion to $2 trillion, depending on the share price and how shares are counted.

  2. 02Field-wide · law

    US senators propose the AUDIT AI Act: listed companies would have to report their AI deals, oversight and risks

    What happenedSenators Mark Warner and Angela Alsobrooks (Democrats) and John Kennedy and Mike Rounds (Republicans) introduced a bill that would ask the US securities regulator (SEC) to write new disclosure rules. Public companies would have to report AI infrastructure financing such as leases and guarantees, say whether their board oversees AI, and add a standard AI risk section covering, for example, dependence on outside models, reliance on a few computing providers and data-rights liabilities. Warner's main concern is "circular" deals, where companies fund firms that are also their customers.

    Why it mattersIf passed, it would create a new, standardised public data source on how organisations depend on AI, useful for research on information systems governance.

    Note: this is a proposal only. We could not read the bill text; the details come from news reports based on Politico.

    Source: IANS via SocialNews.XYZ · 19 Nov 2026, and MLex · Nov 2026

  3. 03Field-wide · security

    European Commission confirms an "AI-generated" cyberattack that took staff systems offline

    What happenedOn 19 November the Commission confirmed that it was hit by what its spokesperson called an "AI-generated cyber campaign", first detected on 22 October. Several services were taken offline as a precaution, affected people were told, and CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor are involved. POLITICO reports that the attackers used ARTEX, an open-source AI hacking tool, and that payroll, leave and medical reimbursement systems were disrupted for more than ten days.

    Why it mattersIt shows that AI tools now let attackers find and exploit weak points in large public organisations quickly, which raises the bar for security in universities and public information systems.

    Note: the Commission did not name the tool or the attackers and says it has no evidence of a wide data breach; the ARTEX detail is reported, not confirmed. The FBI warned last week that ARTEX is used by criminals and state-backed groups.

    Source: The Record · 19 Nov 2026, and Newsweek · 19 Nov 2026

  4. 04Privacy · AI agents

    Meta's Muse agent reportedly mixed up users' conversations and showed private data to the wrong people

    What happenedReuters reviewed internal posts in which Meta employees said the Muse AI agent mixed up conversations between accounts. Examples included childcare requests and product orders the user never made, and a bank balance that was not theirs added to a chat about the weather. Another employee got a reminder to answer a family member's Signal message although they say they had not given Muse access to other apps. Muse launched in September and has more than 10 million downloads.

    Why it mattersPersonal AI agents that read across apps are a new kind of information system, and this case shows why data separation, permissions and audit are central design issues.

    Note: the claims come from employee posts reported by Reuters and have not been independently confirmed; Meta did not immediately comment.

    Source: TechCrunch · 19 Nov 2026, and 9to5Mac · 19 Nov 2026

  5. 05Artificial intelligence · agents

    Google's Gemini app can now use your Mac for you

    What happenedGoogle added "computer use" to the Gemini desktop app for macOS. The agent works in native Mac apps and websites in the background, for example to fill in forms, sort files into folders or build slides from files, while the user watches a live preview and can pause, stop or approve actions. It is available first in English in the US for Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers.

    Why it mattersAll major AI companies now offer agents that act directly on a person's computer, which changes how people use desktop software and raises new questions about control and trust.

    Note: Google has given few details on how data from the screen and files is handled, and no date for other countries.

    Source: TechCrunch · 19 Nov 2026

  6. 06Artificial intelligence · EU policy

    59 European tech leaders warn that Europe "cannot sleep through the AI revolution" and ask for an AI budget

    What happenedAn open letter signed by 59 leaders, including economist Philippe Aghion, Yann LeCun and the heads of DeepL, OVHcloud, ElevenLabs and Lovable, asks the EU to make its Digital Omnibus simplification binding and go further, create a single "28th regime" for companies, open up data access and set aside money for AI in the 2028–2034 budget now being negotiated. The European Commission replied that its tech sovereignty plans need at least €500 billion and that the first part of its vision will come in about two weeks.

    Why it mattersThe EU budget and further changes to the AI Act's rules will decide research funding and compliance work for European universities for years.

    Source: Euronews · 18 Nov 2026, updated 19 Nov 2026

  7. 07Field-wide · data centres

    IEA: data centre emissions could rise by 70% by 2030 as AI grows

    What happenedThe International Energy Agency's latest World Energy Outlook says data centre electricity use could double to about 1,000 TWh by the end of the decade, and emissions could rise by around 70%, because more gas and coal are used. Coal and gas already supply about 60% of the electricity that data centres use; in the US gas may supply about 60% by 2030, while in the EU renewables could reach about two-thirds.

    Why it mattersThe environmental cost of AI and cloud services is becoming a measurable factor in IT procurement and in teaching about sustainable information systems.

    Note: figures are as reported by the trade press; we did not check them in the IEA report itself.

    Source: Data Centre Review · 19 Nov 2026, and Bernama · Nov 2026

    چه اتفاقی افتادتازه‌ترین گزارش چشم‌انداز جهانی انرژی (World Energy Outlook) آژانس بین‌المللی انرژی می‌گوید مصرف برق مراکز داده تا پایان این دهه ممکن است دو برابر شود و به حدود ۱۰۰۰ تراوات‌ساعت برسد، و انتشار کربن آن‌ها به دلیل استفادهٔ بیشتر از گاز و زغال‌سنگ حدود ۷۰ درصد افزایش یابد. زغال‌سنگ و گاز هم‌اکنون حدود ۶۰ درصد برق مراکز داده را تأمین می‌کنند؛ در آمریکا سهم گاز تا ۲۰۳۰ ممکن است به حدود ۶۰ درصد برسد، در حالی که در EU سهم انرژی‌های تجدیدپذیر ممکن است به حدود دو سوم برسد.
