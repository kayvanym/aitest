News

  1. 01Artificial intelligence · tools

    Anthropic launches Claude Ship: AI agents that review, fix and approve code changes

    What happenedAnthropic released Claude Ship, a group of agents for the last steps of software work. Code Review is now generally available, while two agents are in beta: PR Steward, which deals with failing tests, merge conflicts and reviewer comments, and Claude Approvals, which approves routine changes according to a policy the team writes in plain language as a file in its repository. Anthropic advises starting Approvals in an "observe only" mode; the tools are for Team and Enterprise plans on GitHub.

    Why it mattersLetting an AI approve code moves accountability from people to written policies, a core question for IS governance, audit and the design of human control in organisations.

    Source: Anthropic announcement, and TechCrunch · 23 Nov 2026

  2. 02Field-wide · big company decisions

    Amazon confirms it will buy Decart's chip-optimisation technology and hire its founders

    What happenedAmazon announced an agreement to buy the technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack and to bring Decart's three co-founders and its AI researchers into the company, to make AI workloads run better on its own Trainium chips. Amazon gave no price; Calcalist and Bloomberg report about $7.5 billion. The deal still needs approval from regulators.

    Why it mattersBig cloud providers are buying the software layer that makes their own AI chips competitive, which shapes the price and choice of AI computing that universities and companies can buy.

    Note: yesterday we listed this as a report only; the agreement is now confirmed by Amazon, but the price is not. We could not open Amazon's own statement.

    Source: PPC Land · 23 Nov 2026

  3. 03Privacy · data governance

    Google and Apple warn Canada's Senate that a "lawful access" bill puts metadata and encryption at risk

    What happenedAt a Senate committee on 23 November, Google Canada said the bill would make companies keep users' metadata for up to six months (cut from one year in an earlier draft), creating stores of data that attackers would find attractive; a Google security expert pointed to the Salt Typhoon break-in at telecom wiretap systems. Apple said the bill could force companies to drop or weaken end-to-end encryption. The public safety minister says the bill does not weaken privacy protections.

    Why it mattersMandatory metadata retention and encryption backdoors are classic data-governance trade-offs between security and privacy; the Canadian debate is a live case study for teaching.

    Note: the minister's position and the companies' warnings disagree; we could not check the bill text ourselves.

    Source: The Canadian Press (via Kelowna Daily Courier) · 23 Nov 2026

  4. 04Digital libraries · security

    Queens Public Library brings back most digital services three weeks after a ransomware attack

    What happenedQueens Public Library in New York said on 23 November that public computers, printing, databases, e-books, e-audiobooks, its website and its app work again after a ransomware attack that began around 4 November. Interlibrary loan and the online contact form are still down, and the library does not yet know whether personal data was taken. All 66 branches stayed open using manual processes.

    Why it mattersPublic libraries hold patron data and run many vendor systems with small IT teams; this case shows why backup plans and manual fallbacks belong in every digital-library strategy.

    Note: an earlier digest (22 Nov) mentioned the attack; the new information is the recovery and the still-open question about data theft.

    Source: QNS · 23 Nov 2026

  5. 05Field-wide · market

    Customer-service AI company Sierra doubles its value to $30 billion in six months

    What happenedSierra, the AI customer-service company co-founded by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor, raised $1.75 billion led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed, valuing it at $30 billion, up from $15 billion in May. The company says almost half of the Fortune 50 use it and that charging per solved case, instead of per user, doubled its yearly revenue rate to $400 million in five months.

    Why it matters"Pay per outcome" pricing for AI agents changes how organisations buy and measure information systems, and customer service is where agents are replacing human work first.

    Note: the customer and revenue figures are Sierra's own claims.

    Source: TechCrunch · 23 Nov 2026, and Sierra blog

  6. 06Artificial intelligence · research

    Startup says AI agents designed a whole new CPU, "Frostbite", but only in simulation

    What happenedArchitect Labs says its AI agents designed a general-purpose processor with a new instruction set from scratch. In pre-silicon simulation on a 7 nm design, it claims about 4.6 times the performance per watt of a comparable simple CPU on 25 workloads. No chip has been made, and the comparison CPU was not named.

    Why it mattersIf AI agents can design hardware as well as software, the speed of computing progress could rise; for now it is a striking but unchecked claim, a good example for teaching how to read vendor results.

    Note: the results are the company's own and have not been independently verified; we found the story through The Neuron's 23 November roundup.

    Source: Tom's Hardware · reported 23 Nov 2026

    چرا ارزش دیدن داردشاید مروری به‌روز و مفید برای دانشجویانی باشد که تازه با Claude آشنا می‌شوند؛ نتوانستیم کانال را بررسی کنیم، پس با این آگاهی تماشا کنید.
