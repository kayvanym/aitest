  1. 01Privacy · AI agents

    Signal blocks screenshots by default to keep AI assistants out of private chats

    What happenedOn 20 November Signal said it will restrict screenshots on phones by default so that AI agents cannot read users' messages. On iOS 27 a "Block Siri AI" setting is switched on, which also hides chats from screenshots and the app switcher; on Android, Signal turns on its screen security setting when an assistant tries to capture the screen. President Meredith Whittaker called the fix "incomplete", because agents can still reach data stored on the device, and Signal says it cannot give the same protection on macOS.

    Why it mattersAssistants that "see" the screen bypass the security model of individual apps, so who may read which information now has to be decided at the operating-system level.

    Note: Signal named Apple's Siri and Google's Gemini as the main concern. Reuters reported that Meta's Muse agent had read Signal messages through accessibility and screenshot tools; Meta says Muse only uses permissions users grant.

    Source: Reuters via TimesLIVE · 20 Nov 2026, and The Next Web · 20 Nov 2026

  2. 02Artificial intelligence · safety and policy

    US says Trump and Xi agreed that humans must stay in charge of military AI decisions

    What happenedAfter the leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC on 20 November that Presidents Trump and Xi agreed humans should always be involved in military decisions that use AI, and that AI should not act on its own in major military actions. He said this is separate from the two countries' AI competition, and that Nvidia's H200 remains the most advanced chip China can buy with a licence.

    Why it mattersA "human in the loop" commitment between the two biggest AI powers would be a first, and it raises practical questions about how human control over AI systems is defined and audited.

    Note: this rests on Bessent's account only. We found no joint statement and no confirmation from China.

    Source: Reuters via ThePrint · 20 Nov 2026, and Bloomberg · 20 Nov 2026

  3. 03Artificial intelligence · research

    Anthropic mathematician claims he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis; experts have not checked it yet

    What happenedOn 20 November Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted on his personal website that he and Claude have a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most famous open problems in mathematics. The proof was released as computer-checkable Lean code, reported as about 17 million lines, rather than as a normal paper, and Alpöge says he worked through more than 250,000 pages of AI-generated material. He also said he does not yet fully understand the argument himself.

    Why it mattersIf it holds, it would be a landmark for AI in research, and it raises new questions about how science checks, credits and archives results that no human has fully read.

    Note: not verified. Mathematicians quoted range from hopeful to cautious; one says checking it could take months or years. The Clay Mathematics Institute says verification will take time. Anthropic did not comment to AP.

    Source: IBM Think · 20 Nov 2026, and AP via Boston Globe · 21 Nov 2026

  4. 04Artificial intelligence · evaluation

    METR updates its "time horizon" chart: AI keeps improving on checkable tasks, but lags on judgment

    What happenedThe research group METR published a major update to its chart of how long a task (measured in human working time) AI models can complete. It added more and longer tasks, some of them open-ended. On tasks with clear right answers, progress still follows an exponential trend; on judgment-heavy tasks, models do worse than people, and none of the AI-designed evaluations were good enough for METR to use.

    Why it mattersThis chart is widely quoted in debates about AI and work; the new split between verifiable and judgment tasks is a useful lens for teaching how to evaluate AI systems.

    Note: many human-time figures are estimates, and one philosophy task was rated by a single person. Experts disagree on what the results mean.

    Source: MIT Technology Review · 20 Nov 2026, and METR time horizons page

  5. 05Field-wide · security

    Security incident takes US Department of Agriculture payment systems offline

    What happenedMany USDA applications, including financial tools used for payments to farmers, went offline for several days after the department's security centre "uncovered a security incident", according to an internal email reported by Nextgov/FCW on 19 November. All database passwords were reset in all environments, and outages were expected to continue through the week.

    Why it mattersIt is a reminder that large public information systems still depend on basic controls such as credential management, and that poor internal communication makes incidents worse.

    Note: it is not known who was behind the intrusion or whether data was stolen. USDA had not commented publicly.

    Source: Nextgov/FCW · 19 Nov 2026

  6. 06Information retrieval · UX

    Google adds an AI Mode sidebar to the desktop Search homepage worldwide

    What happenedGoogle is rolling out a collapsible sidebar on the desktop Search homepage in all markets. It lets people see and return to their recent AI Mode searches, much like the chat history in an AI assistant. Google's Robby Stein announced it on X; the design had been in testing since late October.

    Why it mattersSearch is moving from one-off queries to ongoing, saved conversations, which changes how people search, how results are reused and what search history reveals about users.

    Note: the source is one official post and one trade-press report.

    Source: Search Engine Roundtable · 19 Nov 2026
