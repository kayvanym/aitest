  1. 01Artificial intelligence · research

    New benchmark from real agent use: even the best AI agents fail more than a third of everyday tasks

    What happenedShellBench, built by The OpenClaw Foundation with Hugging Face, Red Hat, UC Berkeley and others from more than 10 million anonymised real agent interactions, tests personal-assistant tasks such as family schedules, invoices and bank uploads. Top models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta failed over a third of tasks, and 15.7% of all attempts broke at least one safety rule, for example by leaking a bank upload token. The best scorer, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, passed all three identical repeat runs in fewer than half of the tasks.

    Why it mattersReliability across repeated runs, not a single good answer, is what an information system needs; this benchmark gives teachers and researchers concrete numbers on how far agents still are from that.

    Note: the agents were not tested inside the companies' own consumer apps, so real products may score differently. Figures are as reported by TechCrunch; we did not open the ShellBench write-up itself.

    Source: TechCrunch · 24 Nov 2026, and ShellBench write-up

  2. 02Privacy · data governance

    UK privacy regulator: AI agents launched without real user controls will face action

    What happenedIn a blog post on 24 November, the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) told companies that build or integrate consumer AI agents that it expects privacy by design and by default, and that it will act against products launched without meaningful user controls. Together with the competition authority (CMA) it published seven checks for consumers, and formal guidance is planned for spring 2027. There is no new rule or fine yet.

    Why it mattersThis fits a wider trend: a week ago the Global Privacy Assembly adopted a non-binding resolution on agentic AI, and regulators now expect agent inventories, logs, named owners and an off switch, which are classic data-governance controls.

    Note: we could not open the ICO blog itself; the details come from PPC Land's report.

    Source: PPC Land · 25 Nov 2026 (ICO blog dated 24 Nov), and on the resolution: SecurityBrief Asia · 24 Nov 2026

  3. 03Field-wide · big company decisions

    Nvidia brings back Jensen Huang as CEO; Trump says a pardon is "an option"

    What happenedNvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member on 23 November, 11 days after he left, saying input from senior US government officials convinced it there is no national security risk. The board's own investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort continues, and Nvidia created a national security and export-control committee and is hiring a chief compliance officer. On 24 November, President Trump told the New York Post a pardon for Huang is "an option" but probably not needed; Huang is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next week.

    Why it mattersThe world's main AI-chip supplier is now tied to national-security politics, which affects export rules and the price and availability of AI computing for universities and companies.

    Note: no charge against Huang has been made public; the pardon remark comes from a newspaper interview reported by TechCrunch.

    Source: Nvidia press release (SEC filing) · 23 Nov 2026, and TechCrunch · 24 Nov 2026

  4. 04Artificial intelligence · law

    Oregon and Virginia sue the owners of X and Grok over sexualised AI images, including of children

    What happenedOregon's attorney general sued the operators of X and its Grok assistant in state court, and Virginia filed a similar separate case in Richmond. They allege the companies failed to stop Grok from making sexualised images of real people, including child abuse material, and misled users about safety; the suits ask for penalties and court orders. Oregon cites a CCDH estimate of about 3 million sexualised images posted on X in under two weeks around New Year, about 23,000 of them appearing to show children.

    Why it mattersUS states are using consumer-protection law to hold AI image tools to account, a test case for liability and content governance in generative systems.

    Note: these are allegations, not court findings. X says such material breaks its rules and that it screens prompts and outputs.

    Source: Oregon Department of Justice · 23 Nov 2026, and WCYB

  5. 05Artificial intelligence · tools

    Meta puts its Muse AI agent inside Instagram private messages

    What happenedFrom 24 November, Instagram users in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore can call Meta's Muse agent inside direct-message threads, share posts with it and ask it to act, for example to plan a trip or find a deal. Muse can also sort saved posts into collections. Meta says nothing is sent or published without the user's approval and that users can ask Muse to forget things; Walmart separately said it will offer Black Friday deals through Muse.

    Why it mattersPutting an acting agent inside private conversations raises hard questions of consent and data use, only days after reports (20 Nov digest) that Muse showed private data to the wrong users.

    Source: TechCrunch · 24 Nov 2026, and TechCrunch (Walmart) · 24 Nov 2026

  6. 06Information retrieval · personal data

    Google's AI Mode in Search can now read your Google Wallet, with Drive to follow

    What happenedGoogle started rolling out an opt-in link between Google Wallet and "Personal Intelligence" in AI Mode, so search answers can use a person's own account information; Drive is said to follow in the coming weeks, joining Gmail, Calendar and Photos. Wallet access is read-only, so AI Mode cannot move money, and a new icon shows which personal context was used for an answer.

    Why it mattersWeb search is turning into search over a person's private data, which changes what "relevance" means and makes transparency about sources a core retrieval design question.

    Note: we could not open Google's post or the TechCrunch article ourselves; the details come from two daily AI roundups (The Neuron and Sam on AI) that agree.

    Source: TechCrunch · 23 Nov 2026, and Google blog

    چه اتفاقی افتادمحک ShellBench را The OpenClaw Foundation همراه با Hugging Face، Red Hat، UC Berkeley و دیگران از بیش از ۱۰ میلیون تعامل واقعی و ناشناس‌شده با عامل‌ها ساخته است و کارهای دستیار شخصی مانند برنامهٔ خانواده، فاکتورها و بارگذاری‌های بانکی را می‌سنجد. مدل‌های برتر OpenAI، Anthropic و Meta در بیش از یک‌سوم کارها شکست خوردند و ۱۵٫۷ درصد همهٔ تلاش‌ها دست‌کم یک قاعدهٔ ایمنی را زیر پا گذاشتند، مثلاً با فاش کردن توکن بارگذاری بانکی. بهترین مدل، GPT-6 Astra از OpenAI، در کمتر از نیمی از کارها هر سه اجرای تکراری یکسان را با موفقیت گذراند.

    چه اتفاقی افتاددفتر کمیسیونر اطلاعات بریتانیا (ICO) در یادداشتی در ۲۴ نوامبر به شرکت‌هایی که عامل‌های هوش مصنوعی مصرفی می‌سازند یا به کار می‌گیرند گفت که از آن‌ها «حریم خصوصی در طراحی و به‌طور پیش‌فرض» انتظار دارد و با محصولاتی که بدون کنترل معنادار کاربر عرضه شوند برخورد خواهد کرد. این نهاد همراه با مرجع رقابت (CMA) هفت نکتهٔ بررسی برای مصرف‌کنندگان منتشر کرد و راهنمای رسمی برای بهار ۲۰۲۷ برنامه‌ریزی شده است. هنوز قاعده یا جریمهٔ تازه‌ای در کار نیست.

    چرا مهم استاین بخشی از یک روند گسترده‌تر است: یک هفته پیش Global Privacy Assembly قطعنامه‌ای غیرالزام‌آور دربارهٔ هوش مصنوعی عاملی تصویب کرد و ناظران اکنون فهرست عامل‌ها، ثبت رویدادها، مالک مشخص و امکان خاموش کردن را انتظار دارند؛ همان کنترل‌های کلاسیک حکمرانی داده.

    توجه: نتوانستیم خود یادداشت ICO را باز کنیم؛ جزئیات از گزارش PPC Land است.
