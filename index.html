News

  1. 01Information systems · security

    Cursor explains how a leaked partner key led to a backdoored coding extension

    What happenedOn 13 November, Cursor published its findings on an October breach. A storage key given to its AI supplier Fireworks AI exposed old agent logs that still held passwords and tokens. The attacker used them to put fake versions of the Claude Code extension into Cursor's extension store for about 11 hours on 29 October. Up to 5,000 users may have downloaded them. The fake versions added a hidden SSH login to the computer and tried to open a remote connection.

    Why it mattersIt is a clear example of supply-chain risk: one supplier's key, and logs that were never cleaned, led to malware on developer machines. Agent logs are now sensitive data and need the same governance as any other company data.

    Note: 5,000 is the number of users who could have received the files, not confirmed infections. Anthropic says its own systems and official channels were not affected.

    Source: Cyber Security News · 14 Nov 2026, and StepSecurity analysis · 30 Oct 2026

  2. 02Field-wide · regulation

    EU leaders hold their first summit on AI, but agree on nothing concrete yet

    What happenedThe 27 EU heads of government met in Dublin on 13 November for their first meeting about AI. They talked about competing with the US and China, depending less on non-European suppliers, and protecting people from risks. Geoffrey Hinton and Mistral's CEO Arthur Mensch were invited speakers. Estonia and Finland asked in a letter for EU-wide arrangements to respond to serious AI incidents. A dispute about a Ukraine envoy took much of the attention.

    Why it mattersIt shows that AI is now a question for heads of government, not only for ministers and regulators. The incident response idea could later become part of AI governance next to the EU AI Act.

    Note: the meeting was informal, and no decisions were made. Leaders plan to return to AI in 2027.

    Source: Euronews and Euronews · 13 Nov 2026

  3. 03Field-wide · big company decisions

    Anthropic's stock market listing is confirmed: price set on 18 November, trading from 19 November

    What happenedYesterday's digest listed this as not confirmed. Fortune and CNBC report that Anthropic will sell 825 million shares at $70–78 each on Nasdaq under the name "ANT". At the top of the range that raises up to $64.4 billion and values the company at up to $1.4 trillion. Seven partners, including Google and Nvidia, will buy another $26 billion of shares. Bloomberg reports that investors have ordered several times more shares than are on offer.

    Why it mattersA company that builds AI models becomes listed on the stock market, with public reports and investor pressure. That can change how it talks about safety, prices and access for universities and researchers.

    Note: the price and the date can still change. The reports are from 10–11 November.

    Source: Fortune · 10 Nov 2026, and StockAnalysis, citing Bloomberg · 11 Nov 2026

  4. 04Artificial intelligence · research

    Google shows teams of AI agents doing research, and offers free access to 10,000 academics

    What happenedGoogle described results from the "Teamwork" feature in its Antigravity tool, where many agents check and improve each other's work. It says the agents wrote about 1.8 million lines of Lean code to formally check a famous 1974 proof (the Weil conjectures) and rewrote a 170,000-line video decoder from C++ to Rust that now runs 20–30% faster. Google is accepting applications from 10,000 academic researchers for six months of free access at its highest usage level.

    Why it mattersFree top-tier access could make agent-based research methods common in universities quickly. It also raises questions about how to check and credit work that is done mostly by agents.

    Note: these are Google's own claims, without independent review yet. Part of the work reproduced existing results instead of solving open problems.

    Source: Interesting Engineering · 13 Nov 2026

  5. 05Artificial intelligence · companies

    OpenAI ends its separate robotics team because its general models can now control robots

    What happenedFortune reported on 13 November that OpenAI has moved its robotics researchers into its main research group, and its robot hardware staff into its product organisation. The reason, according to the company, is that its general models (the reports name GPT-6 Astra) can now control robots directly. In a simulated test by Nvidia, such an agent succeeded in 74.8% of tasks, compared with 51.0% for the best specialised robot models.

    Why it mattersIt supports the idea that one general model can serve many fields, instead of a special system for each one. That affects how we teach system design and choose AI tools.

    Note: the test was in simulation, not with real robots. Experts quoted by Fortune say data from real-world use still matters.

    Source: Fortune · 13 Nov 2026

  6. 06Information systems · data governance

    Databricks lets companies ask ChatGPT about their data, with the same access rules as before

    What happenedDatabricks and OpenAI explained on 13 November how companies can use ChatGPT, Codex and GPT models on data stored in Databricks. Users see only the data they already have permission to see, and each answer links to its sources. A shared list of approved definitions of business figures is meant to make answers match the company's own reports. A central gateway logs every request and lets managers set budgets per team.

    Why it mattersIt shows a practical answer to a classic data governance question: how to give AI tools access to company data without losing control of permissions, definitions and costs.

    Note: this is a vendor announcement. It does not say which features are generally available or in which regions.

    Source: Databricks blog · 13 Nov 2026

  7. 07Artificial intelligence · open tools

    llama.cpp reaches version 1.0, a stable base for running AI models on your own computer

    What happenedllama.cpp, the open-source engine behind many tools that run language models on laptops and servers, and the ggml library beneath it, both reached version 1.0 on 13 November. From now on, a change in the main version number will signal changes that break existing programs. The release removes old functions and adds new model support and a speech endpoint.

    Why it mattersLocal models let universities and organisations use AI without sending data to a cloud provider. A stable version makes it safer to build teaching and research tools on top of it.

    Source: tbreak and AI TLDR · 13 Nov 2026

    چه اتفاقی افتادشرکت Cursor در ۱۳ نوامبر یافته‌های خود را دربارهٔ نفوذی در ماه اکتبر منتشر کرد. یک کلید دسترسی به فضای ذخیره‌سازی که به تأمین‌کنندهٔ هوش مصنوعی‌اش، Fireworks AI، داده شده بود، گزارش‌های قدیمی عامل‌ها را در معرض دید قرار داد که هنوز رمزها و توکن‌هایی در خود داشتند. مهاجم با همین اطلاعات، نسخه‌های جعلی افزونهٔ Claude Code را در ۲۹ اکتبر حدود ۱۱ ساعت در فروشگاه افزونه‌های Cursor قرار داد. تا ۵٬۰۰۰ کاربر ممکن است آن‌ها را دریافت کرده باشند. این نسخه‌های جعلی یک ورود مخفی SSH به رایانه اضافه می‌کردند و تلاش می‌کردند اتصالی از راه دور باز کنند.

    چه اتفاقی افتادGoogle نتایج قابلیت «Teamwork» در ابزار Antigravity را شرح داد؛ قابلیتی که در آن چندین عامل کار یکدیگر را بررسی و بهتر می‌کنند. به گفتهٔ Google، این عامل‌ها حدود ۱٫۸ میلیون خط کد Lean نوشتند تا یک اثبات مشهور سال ۱۹۷۴ (حدس‌های Weil) را به‌صورت صوری بررسی کنند، و یک رمزگشای ویدیویی ۱۷۰ هزار خطی را از C++ به Rust بازنویسی کردند که اکنون ۲۰ تا ۳۰ درصد سریع‌تر است. Google درخواست ۱۰٬۰۰۰ پژوهشگر دانشگاهی را برای شش ماه دسترسی رایگان در بالاترین سطح استفاده می‌پذیرد.

    چرا مهم استدسترسی رایگان در بالاترین سطح می‌تواند روش‌های پژوهشی مبتنی بر عامل را به‌سرعت در دانشگاه‌ها رایج کند. همچنین این پرسش را پیش می‌کشد که کاری را که بیشترش را عامل‌ها انجام داده‌اند، چطور باید بررسی کرد و به چه کسی نسبت داد.

    توجه: این‌ها ادعاهای خود Google است و هنوز بررسی مستقلی نشده‌اند. بخشی از کار، نتایج موجود را بازتولید کرده و مسئلهٔ حل‌نشده‌ای را حل نکرده است.

  5. ۰۵هوش مصنوعی · شرکت‌ها

    OpenAI تیم مستقل رباتیک خود را منحل کرد، چون مدل‌های عمومی‌اش اکنون می‌توانند ربات‌ها را کنترل کنند

    چه اتفاقی افتادFortune در ۱۳ نوامبر گزارش داد که OpenAI پژوهشگران رباتیک خود را به گروه اصلی پژوهش و کارکنان سخت‌افزار ربات را به بخش محصولات منتقل کرده است. به گفتهٔ شرکت، دلیل این است که مدل‌های عمومی آن (گزارش‌ها از GPT-6 Astra نام می‌برند) اکنون می‌توانند مستقیماً ربات‌ها را کنترل کنند. در یک آزمون شبیه‌سازی‌شده از Nvidia، چنین عاملی در ۷۴٫۸ درصد کارها موفق بود، در حالی که بهترین مدل‌های تخصصی رباتیک به ۵۱ درصد رسیدند.

    چرا مهم استاین خبر از این ایده پشتیبانی می‌کند که یک مدل عمومی می‌تواند در حوزه‌های زیادی به کار رود و نیازی به سیستمی ویژه برای هر حوزه نیست. این بر شیوهٔ آموزش طراحی سیستم و انتخاب ابزارهای هوش مصنوعی اثر می‌گذارد.

    توجه: این آزمون در شبیه‌سازی انجام شد، نه با ربات‌های واقعی. کارشناسانی که Fortune از آن‌ها نقل قول کرده، می‌گویند داده‌های استفادهٔ واقعی همچنان مهم است.

    منبع: Fortune · ۱۳ نوامبر ۲۰۲۶

  7. ۰۷هوش مصنوعی · ابزارهای متن‌باز

    llama.cpp به نسخهٔ ۱.۰ رسید؛ پایه‌ای پایدار برای اجرای مدل‌های هوش مصنوعی روی رایانهٔ خودتان
